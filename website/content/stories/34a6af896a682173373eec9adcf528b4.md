### Part 8: Coming Clean

Marion’s number was on the screen. Sonia had opened it before she knew what she wanted to say, and now her thumb rested beside the call button.

What could she even say?

“Hey, Marion, I can’t stop fantasizing about locking you in a chastity belt and keeping the key while you beg me for release?”

She almost laughed. Marion would probably make a joke about her archaeological kink, then want to hear more. That was what made the call so difficult.

And that was the problem, wasn’t it? It wasn’t just about the acts. Their play—ropes, crops, even the more intense nights with wax or pain—had always been built on trust, care, and an unspoken agreement to never take themselves too seriously. They could play hard, but they always checked in, their connection never faltering. Even during their hardest play, they could make each other laugh.

But this…

Sonia gripped the desk. This wasn’t playful. It wasn’t compassionate. When the hum had her in its grip, when the fantasies took hold, they were nothing like the nights they shared.

There was no humor in them.

In those dreams, Marion wasn’t just her partner—she was her possession. Her body, her mind, her pleasure, her pain—it all belonged to Sonia. She wanted control without interruption.

And Marion’s surrender wasn’t the enthusiastic, consensual submission they shared in their scenes. It was silent and complete. It scared Sonia how much she had enjoyed the thought of it.

The phone screen dimmed beneath her finger. She could tell Marion. Then she would have to hear the words aloud and wait for an answer. There would be no taking them back if Marion heard something in them she had never heard before.

Her gaze drifted to the window, where the late afternoon light filtered through the blinds, casting striped shadows across the room. She could not stop thinking about going further.

Marion’s face flashed in her mind—not the playful, smiling Marion she loved, but the Marion from her dreams. Kneeling. Bound. Looking up at Sonia with wide, pleading eyes.

The thought excited her, then made her feel sick.

This isn’t who I am, she thought, squeezing her eyes shut. This isn’t who we are.

And yet…

She could still remember the hum and Marion’s submission. She closed her laptop and rested her forehead in her hands. She needed to explain the part that frightened her. Marion might hear the things she wanted to do and mistake the confession for an invitation.

Her phone buzzed with a text from Marion. *Dinner tonight? Miss you.*

Sonia typed, *Miss you too. Dinner sounds perfect. Can’t wait.*

She read it again before setting the phone down. Tonight, then. She owed Marion more than that easy answer.

The smell of garlic, butter, and thyme greeted Sonia as she stepped into the apartment. Usually the smell comforted her. Tonight she was too anxious. Marion was at the stove, dressed casually in a loose sweater that hung just off her shoulder, her hair tied back in a messy knot. A bottle of red wine stood open on the counter.

“Perfect timing,” Marion said, flashing Sonia a smile as she stirred something in a pan. “Dinner’s almost ready. How was work?”

“Busy.” Sonia put her bag down and looked at the table rather than Marion. “You’ve outdone yourself.”

Marion grinned, motioning toward the small dining table already set with plates and candles. “It’s chicken, Sonia. And ratatouille.” Marion pointed with the spoon. “The Côtes du Rhône is doing most of the work.”

Sonia felt the corners of her mouth lift despite herself. “You spoil me,” she said softly.

“You deserve it,” Marion replied without hesitation, her voice light but her eyes warm.

The food was good. Sonia knew it was good, though she would have struggled to describe it. She kept finding one more ordinary thing to say before the thing she had come home to tell Marion.

When her plate was empty, Sonia pushed it away. Marion was still holding her glass. Their eyes met, and this time Sonia did not look for something else on the table.

Sonia broke the silence first, her voice soft. “Marion, we need to talk.”

Marion looked at her. “I wondered how long you were going to make me wait. What is it?”

For a moment Sonia could only look at her. “The collar,” she said. “What happened yesterday. I haven’t stopped thinking about it.”

Marion tilted her head slightly, her brow furrowing. “Okay,” she said, her tone cautious but open.

Sonia inhaled deeply, gathering her thoughts. “Yesterday, when I put the collar on you… it did something to me. To us. I felt… different. Like I wasn’t just your partner anymore. I was…” She paused, searching for the right words. “I was your everything. Your entire world. And I loved it.”

Marion’s fingers loosened round her glass. She waited.

Sonia went on. “But it wasn’t just that. It wasn’t playful. It wasn’t us. I didn’t feel like I was sharing something with you—I felt like I was taking something. Everything. It was intoxicating, Marion. And it scared me.”

Marion set her glass down carefully, folding her hands in her lap. “And that didn’t feel like loving me?” Marion asked.

Sonia nodded. “It felt like power. Like control. And I keep thinking about it—about you, collared and bound, looking up at me like I’m the only thing that matters in the world. I keep imagining… things. Dark things. And I don’t want to feel that way. Not about you. Not about us.”

Marion took a moment to answer. “I think I know.”

“You do?”

Marion nodded, smiling wryly. “The collar did something to me too,” she admitted. “When you took it off, I felt… empty. Everything’s been dull since then. The colors aren’t as bright. The world doesn’t feel as real. And it’s not just that I miss wearing it—it’s what I felt when I had it on. That sense of… devotion. Of security. Like nothing could go wrong because you were in control, and all I had to do was obey.”

Sonia listened.

Marion looked down at her hands, her fingers curling slightly. “I know where it comes from,” she said. “I keep telling myself that. The collar made me feel it.” She looked up. “It doesn’t help. I still miss it.”

Neither spoke for a while.

Finally, Sonia spoke,. “I manipulated the database today,” she admitted. “I erased some records and hid some artifacts. More restraints. A belt, shackles, thigh bands. I told myself I might not do anything with them, that it could all be a mistake, but… I knew what I was doing. And I knew why. I’ve been thinking about you. About what it would feel like to see you like that. To have you like that.”

Marion looked up meeting Sonia’s eyes. There was no judgment there, only understanding.

“I don’t know what’s happening to us,” Sonia said. “But I’m scared, Marion. Of what I’m feeling. Of what this might do to us.”

Marion came round the table and knelt beside Sonia’s chair, taking her hands.

“You should have told me,” she said. “I should have told you how much I missed it. We can start there.” Her thumbs moved over Sonia’s knuckles. “Together. As equals. Can we do that?”

Sonia nodded and pulled Marion close. She held her until she felt calmer.

For a moment, Sonia stopped thinking about the collar. That night, the bedside lamp lit the quiet bedroom. Sonia and Marion lay together under the quilt, warm against each other. The air smelled faintly of lavender from the diffuser Marion had turned on earlier, its calming scent blending with the faint crispness of clean sheets.

Marion rested her head on Sonia’s chest, her arm draped loosely across Sonia’s waist. Sonia’s hand moved slowly, rhythmically, through Marion’s hair, her fingers threading through the soft curls. Neither tried to fill the silence.

Sonia pressed a soft kiss to Marion’s forehead, her lips lingering for a moment. Marion responded by snuggling closer, her nose brushing Sonia’s collarbone as she sighed contentedly.

“I’m here,” Sonia whispered, her voice barely audible.

Marion answered with a quiet hum and traced small circles on Sonia’s side. Neither tried to turn the touch into anything more.

Their legs tangled under the quilt, bare skin brushing bare skin, but there was no urgency in the contact. She was glad to be close. Marion shifted slightly, her breath soft against Sonia’s skin as she murmured, “Thank you.”

Sonia didn’t ask what for—she knew.

She tilted her head, resting her cheek against Marion’s hair. “Always,” she replied quietly, holding Marion close.

They lay together until they slept.
