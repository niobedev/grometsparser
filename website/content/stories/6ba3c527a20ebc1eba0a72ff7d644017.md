### Part 1: The Dig

Sonia crouched by the edge of the trench, brushing sand off what looked like the base of a carved column. The Canary sun burned overhead, but she barely noticed. Geometric shapes emerged beneath her brush, crossed by flowing, almost organic lines. None of it matched anything she had seen in the textbooks. It wasn’t Roman or Phoenician. It didn’t resemble Guanche work either.

“Sonia!” Nate called from the other end of the dig. “Leave that a minute. Come and look.”

Sonia set down her brush reluctantly. She brushed her gloves together, adjusted her straw hat, and picked her way across the trenches. Half-excavated walls broke the slope above her, their upper courses still buried in ash.

“What is it?” she asked as she reached Nate.

He pointed to a slab of stone they had uncovered the day before, now resting on a makeshift frame of wooden beams. Cleaning had revealed carvings in rows and columns, perhaps a form of writing. But it wasn’t the carvings that drew her attention—it was the inlay. Metal filled the grooves, glinting in the sun.

“Tell me that doesn’t look like a circuit,” Nate said.

Sonia bent closer. The metal felt cool through her glove as she traced one of the lines.

“Decoration, perhaps. But look at the symmetry.” She followed it to the next junction. “Why be so precise?”

“It gets weirder,” Nate said. He gestured toward another section of the site, where workers were uncovering wide, shallow steps. “That’s the entrance to the main complex. Just got a glimpse inside—there’s something down there you should see.”

Sonia followed him, her boots crunching over the gritty soil. The staircase descended into shadow, flanked by walls carved with more of the strange patterns. Lanterns lit the rough-hewn walls below.

Sonia stopped at the chamber entrance. The air was heavy, with the metallic tang of an approaching storm. The room was circular, its walls lined with niches that held cracked and broken objects—ceramic bowls, glass vials, things that might once have been tools. At the center of the room stood a dais, its surface smooth and black, as if carved from obsidian.

“This,” Nate said, “is what I wanted you to see.”

He pointed to a panel set into the wall near the dais. Unlike the other carvings, the panel was clean and glowed blue-green. Squares divided its surface, each bearing more symbols. As Sonia approached, she felt a faint hum beneath her feet, like the vibration of a running machine.

“This isn’t possible,” she murmured. “This has to be some kind of hoax.”

“Then someone’s gone to a lot of trouble,” Nate said. “How do you explain the panel?”

Sonia reached out, hesitating before her fingers touched the glowing panel. The air around it seemed warmer, buzzing faintly against her skin. “If this is Atlantean…” She stopped. The discovery could overturn their account of ancient history.

Around the dais, shallow depressions formed a circle. Sonia looked from one to the next. Someone could have stood in each—or knelt.

“What do you think this was for?” she asked, her voice low.

Nate shrugged uneasily. “Doesn’t feel like a temple, does it?”

“No,” Sonia agreed. “It doesn’t.”

Sonia studied the glowing lines in the walls. They seemed to shift in the lantern light. She doubted anyone had made this place for worship or art. Whatever its purpose, it troubled her.

The following day, Sonia crouched in the shade of the tarpaulin, brushing sand off another piece of metal that had emerged from the soil. Someone had shaped its curves and hollows with care. She tilted it in the sunlight, revealing a faint seam along one edge. Perhaps it was a hinge; the corrosion made it hard to tell. Her brush exposed a blackened surface, too worn to reveal much detail.

She worked a little apart from the main excavation. The others were too focused on the inscriptions in proto-Linear A to pay much attention to her quiet work. She didn’t mind. She wasn’t a linguist. And if she was right about what she’d found, this could be just as significant.

Round and half-round fragments filled her tray. Their edges and curves suggested parts of a mechanism. A few had empty sockets where stones might once have been set. She imagined them colorful and polished, though now they were as blackened as everything else.

Her brush stopped at two larger pieces. She studied the curves, fitting them together in her mind. A collar. Or something very much like one.

Jewelry, she told herself. The workmanship was elaborate enough. But she kept turning the pieces over to examine the fastening.

She ought to put it in the notebook. Give it a number, bag the pieces, let the team examine them. Instead she held them a moment longer. Nearly intact. Small enough to take home.

She glanced over her shoulder. Nate was still in the main dig site, debating with one of the linguists about the implications of the proto-Linear A inscriptions. The nearby workers sifted soil for smaller finds. No one was watching her.

She slipped the pieces into her satchel, between layers of cloth so they would not clink. Her heart thudded against her ribs as she straightened up. She was careful with finds. Everyone knew that. And there were so many fragments here; this one was hardly exceptional. She had just spent several minutes telling herself the opposite.

Still, her hands trembled slightly as she turned back to her tray. She brushed more sand away, working more slowly now, her mind distracted. If someone found out… No. No one would find out. It was just a souvenir. Something to remember the dig by. Something to prove to herself, years from now, that she had been here.

When she packaged the rest of her finds, Sonia cataloged each piece in her notebook. She even lingered a little longer over the fragments with empty sockets, their jewel settings blackened and unrecognizable. She recorded it all, as thoroughly as always.

Except for one.

The artifacts piled up day after day, each new discovery stranger than the last. Sonia sat cross-legged on the tarp-covered ground near her section of the dig, her gloves dusty as she examined the latest batch of finds. None of the designs resembled anything she had studied. The team called them Atlantean for want of a better name.

Carla, one of the field techs, held up a delicate frame of metal loops.

“A chastity belt with a circuit board,” she said. “Please tell me I’m wrong.”

Sonia glanced over, suppressing a smile. Carla wasn’t entirely wrong. The ancient artifacts looked strangely modern, with the precision of machine parts. They found arm bands that looked more like restraints, rings with mechanisms no one could figure out, and strange structures with grooves that might have been meant to lock something in place.

Nate uncovered one of the strangest items. He held it up as Sonia approached. The object resembled a collar, not unlike the fragments Sonia had found earlier, but this one was almost intact. Dull black stones studded its curved surface. They might once have been gemstones.

“Check this out,” Nate said, handing it over. “Doesn’t it look… I don’t know… too functional to be just jewelry?”

Sonia turned it over in her hands, her brow furrowing. The inside of the collar had faint ridges, almost like contact points. She ran her thumb over them, her gloved hand catching on something that might once have been a hinge. “It’s not just decorative,” she said finally. “This had a purpose. Something practical.”

“Or sinister,” Carla quipped, tossing a shard of glass into a labeled bag. “I mean, half of this stuff looks like it came from some kind of ancient BDSM dungeon.”

Nate folded his arms. “The cuffs yesterday, this collar, now that frame. We can’t keep calling everything ceremonial.”

“It doesn’t fit any of the cultures we know,” Sonia added. She set the collar down carefully and gestured toward the growing collection of artifacts. “The designs are too advanced for Mycenaean or Minoan, but the aesthetic isn’t like anything else. It’s almost—”

“Alien?” Nate offered, grinning.

Sonia rolled her eyes. “Not alien. Just different. These people—whoever they were—they weren’t like anyone else. They must have thought differently.”

“Apparently the dress code was quite strict,” Carla said.

Sonia ignored her, uneasy. She had been trying to avoid thinking about the implications of the artifacts—the collars, the restraints, the way so many of the designs seemed to straddle the line between function and fetish. It was easier to focus on cataloging, documenting, analyzing. But she still wanted answers.

As the dig progressed, more of the complex came into focus. They uncovered what appeared to be a market square, with rows of stalls marked by low stone walls. Volcanic ash had preserved the stalls and their contents. Sonia stared at metal and stone racks that might once have displayed goods—or something else.

The temple itself revealed even stranger secrets. The carvings inside depicted scenes that were difficult to interpret—figures in elaborate headpieces surrounded by glowing objects, others kneeling or bound in strange devices. Sonia could not separate the sexual scenes from the arrangements of power. Again and again, one figure stood while the others knelt or waited in restraints.

The team argued over its meaning. Some thought it was a religious site, others a center for trade or governance. Sonia wasn’t sure. These people had built the devices for a purpose Sonia still could not grasp.

She couldn’t shake the feeling that the place wasn’t just about worship or trade. It was about control. But control of what—or who—remained a mystery.
