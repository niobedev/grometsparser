### Part 6: The Secret Unlocked

Patches of reflected color lay on the living-room walls. The collar was still where Marion had left it, humming among the ordinary things on the table.

Marion stirred first, stretching lazily before glancing at the collar. She blinked, rubbing her eyes. “It’s so… pretty,” she murmured, her voice still thick with sleep.

Sonia sat up and followed her gaze. “You said ‘dangerous’ yesterday.”

Marion turned to her, a faint smile playing on her lips. “We don’t know that.”

“You were the one warning me.” Sonia meant to leave it there. Instead she kept looking at the clasp. “You already tried it, though.”

Marion nodded hesitantly, her cheeks flushing slightly. “Yeah. But it didn’t do anything. It won’t close. I told you.”

Sonia swung her legs over the edge of the couch, standing and motioning for Marion to do the same. “Let’s try again. This time with me here.” She would watch what happened. That was the difference, she told herself: Marion would not be alone this time. One of them would remain clear-headed. It was usually her.

Marion nodded eagerly, retrieving the collar carefully, wrapping it in cloth. Sonia watched closely as Marion stepped in front of the mirror, lifting the collar to her neck. She hesitated for a moment, then snapped it shut around her throat. The hum grew louder at the contact.

But the clasp didn’t lock. It clicked shut, but when Marion tugged lightly, it fell open again.

“See?” Marion said, her tone tinged with both relief and frustration. “It doesn’t work. It doesn’t—”

“Wait,” Sonia interrupted, taking the collar from Marion’s hands. She turned it over in thought. “What’s a slave without an owner?” she murmured.

Marion tilted her head, confused. “What are you talking about?”

Sonia looked up, her gaze steady. “Of course it won’t lock on its own. It’s not supposed to. Show me your neck.”

Marion’s breath caught, her pulse quickening as she nodded silently and turned to face Sonia, tilting her chin up slightly and holding her hair out of the way. Sonia stepped closer, holding the collar carefully. She could feel its hum vibrating faintly through the cloth. Slowly, she raised it to Marion’s neck.

The moment the collar touched her skin, the hum intensified, and Marion shivered involuntarily. Sonia snapped the halves together, and this time, there was a distinct click. The lock engaged. The collar stayed shut.

At the same time, a small metallic ring embedded into the clasp fell loose, landing in Sonia’s palm. She stared at it, her heart pounding.

Marion touched the collar tentatively, her fingers brushing over the smooth surface. “It… locked,” she whispered, her voice filled with equal parts awe and trepidation.

Sonia nodded, her gaze fixed on the ring in her hand. “It locked,” she repeated softly. The stronger hum filled the room.

Marion turned to Sonia, her eyes wide. “What now?”

Sonia looked from the ring to Marion.

“Now we find out.”

Sonia watched the blue jewels pulse against Marion’s throat. The rhythm was slow enough to count. She kept her voice level.

“Kneel,” Sonia said.

Marion stayed where she was. She opened her mouth, thought better of whatever she had been about to say, and looked at Sonia.

“Am I supposed to feel something?” she asked. “Because I don’t.”

Sonia frowned thoughtfully, then looked down at the ring in her palm. Slowly, she slipped it onto her finger. The moment the cool metal touched her skin, the hum in the room shifted, growing slightly louder, more focused. Her hand tingled. She felt a bond form between the ring and the collar, between herself and Marion.

“Marion,” she said again, her voice carrying more authority now. “Kneel.”

This time, something changed. Marion froze, her body stiffening slightly as if she had felt an invisible push. The hum seemed to surround her, pressing gently but insistently. Her breathing hitched as the air around her felt heavier, disorienting. She shook her head as if trying to clear it and took a tentative step forward instead of kneeling.

The pull intensified.

“It’s… hard,” Marion said, her voice strained. “Like… like everything is wrong unless…” She trailed off, frowning as she took another step, her movements hesitant and slow.

Sonia watched Marion struggle. Each step took more strength than the last. Marion’s face tightened with concentration, her hands curling into fists as she pushed against the invisible pressure. The hum grew louder, disorienting and unrelenting, and the faint pull became more pronounced, almost like a physical weight pressing on her.

“I can still choose,” Marion said. She had to stop for breath. “It’s just such hard work.”

She took another step, but her movements were sluggish, her breaths coming faster. She hesitated, then bent her knee slightly, as if testing something. The moment she did, the hum softened, the pressure easing just enough for her to catch her breath. Marion’s eyes widened, and she straightened again, only to feel the pull intensify once more.

Each movement against the pull took more effort, until she trembled. The hum made it harder to concentrate. Her limbs felt heavy, her mind foggy.

Finally, Marion let out a shaky breath and gave in. She dropped to her knees.

The moment her knees hit the floor, the hum changed again, softening into something gentle, almost melodic. The tension in the air vanished, and Marion let out a soft, involuntary sigh. The colors around her seemed brighter, her mind clearing. Happiness bubbled through her, warm and unbidden. She knelt further, assuming a pose of reverent attention, her hands resting on her thighs, her head slightly bowed. Everything felt right, as if this was exactly where she was meant to be.

Almost five minutes. Sonia had watched Marion fight for almost five minutes, and now Marion was looking at her as if nothing had been wrong. No reproach. No request to stop.

Sonia crouched in front of Marion, her hand hovering near the collar as her gaze searched her girlfriend’s face. Her cheeks were flushed, her eyes wide and shining as they looked at Sonia. There was no sign of the earlier struggle, only peace and contentment.

“How do you feel?” Sonia asked softly.

Marion smiled faintly, her voice barely above a whisper. “Good. It feels… so good. Only Obedience feels so good.” Her expression softened further, her gaze unwavering. “I’m a good girl.”

Sonia had expected a joke, perhaps a complaint about her knees. Marion simply waited for her next word.

Sonia studied Marion, who was still kneeling reverently before her. The collar gleamed under the morning sun, pulsing faintly with its soft heartbeat rhythm. Even when not commanding, she could feel the bond, and her power over Marion, she could see the devotion in her eyes. This is what an ancient goddess must have felt when her worshippers knelt before her altar, hands raised in supplication. Sonia knew why her heart was racing. She did not like to admit it.

She swallowed hard and asked, “Can you take it off?”

Marion blinked slowly, her head tilting slightly, as if the question didn’t quite register. “Take what off?” she asked, her voice serene and distant.

“The collar,” Sonia clarified. “Can you take it off?”

Marion’s hands closed over her thighs.

“No. Not that.” Her voice rose. “Please, Sonia. Don’t.”

Sonia recoiled at Marion’s desperation. “Marion—”

“It’s not meant to… please don’t.” Marion’s words spilled out in a rush, her hands clenching into fists against her thighs as she looked up at Sonia, her eyes wide and imploring. “Please, Sonia. Don’t take it off.”

Sonia had only asked a question. Now Marion looked close to tears, and some part of her still wanted to hear another obedient answer. She did not trust herself to speak at once.

“Bend your neck, please,” Sonia said firmly, masking her unease.

Marion hesitated for a moment. Her lips were quivering, but she obediently lowered her head. There was no way for her not to. The hum of the collar seemed to grow louder, echoing in the silence between them.

Sonia stepped closer, her hands steady despite the unease in her chest. She slid the ring off her finger and pressed it gently against the collar. It aligned perfectly with a small recess near the clasp, sinking into place where it belonged. There was a soft click, and the collar sprang open.

Sonia pulled it away quickly, wrapping it into its protective cloth again. The hum stopped. Neither woman spoke.

Marion lifted her head slowly, her expression dazed and hollow. Her eyes followed the wrapped collar as Sonia stepped back. Without it, her posture slumped slightly, and her movements seemed heavier, less deliberate. She looked at Sonia, then back at the collar, her gaze filled with longing.

“Put it back?” Marion whispered. When Sonia did not answer, she tried again. “Please.”

The collar was still in Sonia’s hands. She would only have to turn it round, lift it once more. Marion was asking her to.

Instead, she turned away, walking to her desk. She opened the drawer, carefully setting the collar inside, and closed it with a deliberate finality. The drawer lock clicked. Sonia pocketed the key and gripped it.

When she turned back to Marion, she saw the emptiness in her eyes. Marion’s shoulders sagged, and she sat back on her heels, staring at the floor. “It’s just… everything feels so dull,” Marion murmured, her voice breaking slightly. “Without it, the world is… colorless. Flat. I miss it.”

Sonia took a deep breath, fighting the weight in her chest. She had felt it too—the rush, the power. The way the collar seemed to amplify everything. Controlling Marion while she wore it had felt… intoxicating. The raw, unfiltered submission, the connection between them, *owning* her completely—it had felt like nothing else.

But it was wrong.

Or was it?

Sonia touched the key in her pocket. She had put the collar away because of what it was doing to Marion. That was true. It was also easier to think about Marion’s longing than her own.

Sonia stepped closer to Marion, kneeling to take her face gently in her hands. “We’re done with it for now,” she said softly. “You’re still you, Marion. With or without it.”

Marion nodded faintly, but her eyes flicked once more toward the desk, her longing unhidden. Sonia kissed her forehead, holding her close, but her own thoughts churned. Even with the collar locked away, she kept thinking about it.

Later that evening, Sonia lay in their four-poster bed, her arm draped protectively around Marion, who nestled close, her head resting on Sonia’s chest. Marion’s breath came slow and steady, her body warm and pliant in the stillness of the night. Sonia stared up at the ceiling, her fingers trailing absently through Marion’s hair. She remembered Marion begging for the collar, then looking so empty without it.

She missed their connection too.

She found the key through her pocket and pressed her thumb against its teeth. The drawer was locked. Yet she could remember exactly how Marion had looked up at her: no distraction, no hesitation, nothing else she wanted. Sonia had never had anyone’s attention so completely.

The memory came unbidden: Marion kneeling, eyes wide, radiating a devotion so complete it sent a shiver down Sonia’s spine. She wanted that surrender again.

And it was wrong.

“To control others, you must first learn to control yourself,” her mother had once said. “That’s what women do—we bring life into the world, nurture it, and teach it values. But to do that, we first have to shape ourselves. Control starts here.” She had tapped Sonia’s chest. “Inside. If you can’t shape your own self, how can you hope to shape anything else?”

Those lessons had shaped Sonia’s life, her ethics, her approach to her relationship with Marion. She valued balance, structure, and care. Even in their most intense moments of play—ropes tight against skin, the crack of a crop—there was always trust. There was always choice. Their dynamic wasn’t about absolute control; it was about crafting something safe, something mutually fulfilling.

But the collar was different.

It threatened the trust they had built. It offered power Marion could not question. It demanded surrender—not from Marion, but from Sonia herself. It urged her to abandon her rules and take control without answering to Marion.

And it felt good. Too good.

Sonia closed her eyes, the key digging into her palm now. She wanted its power. That frightened her.

She exhaled sharply, her body tense against the soft comfort of the bed. She gripped the key harder. She couldn’t give in. Not to that. Not to it.

Marion shifted against her in her sleep. Sonia focused on that—the steady rhythm of Marion’s breathing, the trust implicit in her touch. She couldn’t let the collar warp what they had built together. She wouldn’t.

When Sonia slept, she dreamed restlessly. Dreams of taking what was not given, of violent domination and absolute power.

Sonia woke with a start, her chest heaving, her body covered in a fine sheen of sweat. Marion was still asleep beside her, curled into her side, her breath warm against Sonia’s skin. Sonia’s hand flew to her pocket, gripping the key tightly as her mind raced. She remembered the dream and shuddered.

She looked down at Marion, her peaceful face glowing faintly in the morning light, and whispered, “What are you doing to me?” The words were meant for the collar, but she wasn’t sure if she was asking it—or herself.
